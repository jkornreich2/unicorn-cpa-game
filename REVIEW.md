### far-001 · Contract liabilities (Seed)

**Not reviewed**

*Maya, CEO:*

> First real customer! They wired $120,000 today, December 1, for a 12-month subscription starting now. Book it so the dashboard shows it.

**Question:** Record the cash receipt on December 1.

**Correct entry:** Dr Cash $120,000 / Cr Deferred Revenue $120,000

Accounts offered: Cash, Accounts Receivable, Subscription Revenue, Deferred Revenue, Prepaid Expenses, Additional Paid-in Capital

**Explanation shown to player:** Cash arrived before the service was delivered, so Kestrel owes the customer twelve months of access. That obligation is a contract liability. Revenue is recognized over time: $10,000 by December 31.

**Exam takeaway:** ASC 606: recognize revenue when (or as) the performance obligation is satisfied. Cash timing does not drive revenue.

> We granted 120,000 options to the new engineering hires on January 1. The valuation firm puts fair value at $5 per option on the grant date. They vest at the end of four years of service.

**Question:** How much compensation expense does Kestrel record in year 1?

**Correct answer:** $150,000

**Explanation shown to player:** Total cost is 120,000 x $5 = $600,000, measured at grant-date fair value and spread straight-line over the four-year service period: $150,000 a year. Debit Compensation Expense, credit APIC (stock options).

**Exam takeaway:** ASC 718: equity awards are measured once, at grant date. Later changes in the share price do not change the expense.

### far-003 · Leases (Series A)

**Not reviewed**

*Dev, CTO:*

> Signed the new office. Five years, $100,000 due at the end of each year, and our borrowing rate is 6%. The present value factor for an ordinary annuity, five periods at 6%, is 4.2124.

**Question:** What lease liability goes on the balance sheet at commencement?

**Correct answer:** $421,240 (accepts ±$60)

**Explanation shown to player:** The liability is the present value of the payments: $100,000 x 4.2124 = $421,240. With no prepaid rent or initial direct costs, the right-of-use asset starts at the same amount.

**Exam takeaway:** ASC 842: lessees put both operating and finance leases on the balance sheet. Only short-term leases (12 months or less) can stay off.

> Enterprise deal closed: $300,000 for a one-year platform subscription plus our implementation service. Sold separately, the subscription goes for $270,000 and implementation for $90,000. Both are distinct.

**Question:** How much of the price is allocated to implementation?

**Correct answer:** $75,000

**Explanation shown to player:** Allocate the transaction price by relative standalone selling price. Implementation is 90,000 of 360,000, or 25%, so it gets $300,000 x 25% = $75,000. The subscription gets $225,000.

**Exam takeaway:** ASC 606 step 4: the discount is spread proportionally across obligations unless evidence shows it belongs to specific ones.

**Not reviewed**

*Priya, Northlight partner:*

> Diligence question. Your office manager sets up new vendors, enters the bills, and releases the payments. That's one person. What are you going to change?

**Question:** Which change addresses the biggest risk?

- [ ] Require her to take a yearly vacation
- [x] **Have someone else approve new vendors and release payments** ← correct
- [ ] Reconcile the bank account quarterly instead of monthly
- [ ] Move the accounting system to the cloud

**Explanation shown to player:** One person who can create a vendor and pay it can pay a fictitious vendor. Splitting authorization and custody of cash from recordkeeping removes that opportunity.

**Exam takeaway:** Keep three duties apart: Authorization, Recordkeeping, Custody of assets.

### aud-002 · Audit risk model (Series A)

**Not reviewed**

*Sam, audit senior:*

> First-year audit, fast growth, and your controls are still thin. We've assessed both inherent risk and control risk as high.

**Question:** How does the audit team respond?

- [ ] Accept higher detection risk and do less substantive testing
- [x] **Set detection risk lower and do more substantive testing** ← correct
- [ ] Raise materiality so fewer items need testing
- [ ] Rely on controls testing instead of substantive procedures

**Explanation shown to player:** Audit risk = risk of material misstatement x detection risk. When the first part is high, the auditor must hold detection risk low, which means more evidence: larger samples, more reliable procedures, and testing closer to year-end.

**Exam takeaway:** The auditor controls only detection risk. Inherent and control risk belong to the client and are assessed, not set.

### aud-003 · Revenue fraud (IPO)

**Not reviewed**

*Sam, audit senior:*

> Sales booked a $900,000 contract on December 31. I'm hearing there may be a side letter that lets the customer cancel through March.

**Question:** Which procedure best tests this?

- [ ] Recalculate the invoice math
- [ ] Trace delivery records forward to recorded sales
- [x] **Confirm contract terms, including side agreements, with the customer** ← correct
- [ ] Compare this year's gross margin with last year's

**Explanation shown to player:** A side letter lives outside the accounting records, so evidence from the customer is the most direct way to find it. Tracing from delivery records to sales tests completeness, which is the wrong direction for overstated revenue.

**Exam takeaway:** Revenue recognition is a presumed fraud risk. Overstatement: vouch from the ledger back to support. Understatement: trace from source forward.

### reg-001 · Entity choice (Seed)

**Not reviewed**

*Maya, CEO:*

> Northlight wants to lead our seed. Their fund has pension plans and foreign investors as limited partners, and they want preferred stock. We're still an LLC taxed as a partnership. What do we become?

**Question:** Which entity fits a venture-backed company?

- [ ] S corporation, to keep one level of tax
- [x] **Delaware C corporation** ← correct
- [ ] Stay an LLC taxed as a partnership
- [ ] General partnership with the fund as a partner

**Explanation shown to player:** A C corporation can issue preferred stock and blocks pass-through income from reaching the fund's tax-exempt and foreign investors. An S corporation fails twice: it allows only one class of stock, and a venture fund (a partnership) cannot be a shareholder.

**Exam takeaway:** S corp limits: 100 shareholders, one class of stock, no partnership, corporate or nonresident alien shareholders.

### reg-002 · Section 83(b) (Seed)

**Not reviewed**

*Dev, CTO:*

> I just bought my 4,000,000 founder shares at $0.001 each, which is what they're worth today. They vest over four years. Our lawyer said something about an 83(b) election?

**Question:** When must Dev file the election?

- [ ] With his tax return for the year of grant
- [ ] Within 30 days of each vesting date
- [x] **Within 30 days of the share transfer** ← correct
- [ ] Within 75 days of the share transfer

**Explanation shown to player:** The election is due within 30 days of the transfer and cannot be extended. Dev is taxed now on value minus price paid, which is $0. Without it, he has ordinary income at every vesting date on the shares' value at that time.

**Exam takeaway:** 83(b): taxed at grant on FMV less amount paid. Later growth is capital gain. If the shares are forfeited, no deduction for tax already paid.

### reg-003 · Stock options (Series A)

**Not reviewed**

*Lena, staff engineer:*

> I'm exercising 10,000 nonqualified options today. Exercise price is $1 and the 409A value is $9 a share. HR says payroll needs something from finance.

**Question:** What is the tax result on the exercise date?

- [ ] No income until she sells the shares
- [ ] $80,000 of long-term capital gain for Lena
- [x] **$80,000 of ordinary income for Lena, and Kestrel deducts $80,000** ← correct
- [ ] $90,000 of ordinary income for Lena, and no deduction for Kestrel

**Explanation shown to player:** For a nonqualified option, the spread at exercise, (9 - 1) x 10,000 = $80,000, is wages to the employee, subject to withholding. The company deducts the same amount. Her basis in the shares becomes $9.

**Exam takeaway:** ISO contrast: no regular tax at exercise (the spread is an AMT item) and no company deduction unless there is a disqualifying disposition.

### reg-004 · Net operating losses (IPO)

**Not reviewed**

*Maya, CEO:*

> We finally made money. Taxable income before any loss deduction is $2,000,000, and we're carrying $5,000,000 of net operating losses from the last few years. So no tax, right?

**Question:** What is Kestrel's federal income tax for the year?

**Correct answer:** $84,000

**Explanation shown to player:** Losses from years after 2017 can offset only 80% of taxable income: $1,600,000. That leaves $400,000 taxed at 21%, which is $84,000. The remaining $3,400,000 carries forward indefinitely.

**Exam takeaway:** Post-2017 NOLs: no carryback, unlimited carryforward, 80% of taxable income cap.
